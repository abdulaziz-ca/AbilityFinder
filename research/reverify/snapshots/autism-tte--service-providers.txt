A clear conversation, supported by a written service agreement or contract, helps establish shared expectations and a positive working relationship. Important topics to discuss includes: Your role as part of the child’s support team, including how your services complement the supports provided by family members, educators, health professionals, and other service providers involved in the child or youth’s care. Your training, qualifications, experience, and approach to supporting children, youth, and families, including any areas of specialization and how you work collaboratively to support family-identified goals and priorities. The family's goals, priorities, and desired outcomes for their child or youth, how progress will be monitored, and how families will lead decisions about services and any adjustments needed over time. Service arrangements , including the frequency, duration, and location of services, determined in partnership with the family. Your fees and invoicing practices , including rates, invoicing, payment timelines, and cancellation and missed appointment policies. Providers should share invoices with families for review before submission so families understand and agree with the services billed and associated costs. Communication expectations , including how and when you will communicate with the family, respond to questions or concerns, and support family-led discussions and decisions about the child's progress. Confidentiality, privacy, and information sharing practices , including circumstances in which information may be shared with other members of the child’s support team, with the family's consent or as otherwise required by law. Processes for addressing concerns or making changes to services, including how service plans, schedules, or goals can be reviewed and adjusted if the child’s or family's needs change. Taking time to discuss these topics at the outset helps establish clear expectations, promotes transparency, and supports a positive and collaborative working relationship with families. Ongoing Communication with Families Regular communication helps families make informed decisions about their child's services and funding. Service providers should keep families informed about: The services being provided. The services and amounts being billed. The amount of funding used and the remaining balance. Anticipated service costs and any expected changes to services or rates. Any factors that may affect access to services within available funding. Families should have opportunities to ask questions, discuss concerns, and share changes to their child's needs or goals. Clear communication helps prevent misunderstandings, supports transparency, and ensures families have the information they need to manage their child's services and funding. Providing Services through the B.C. Children and Youth Disability Benefit Expand All  |  Collapse All   Before Services Can Begin For each funding period, families work with their chosen service providers to complete a Request for Services (RFS) form. The RFS identifies: The maximum hourly or daily rate that can be paid for a service The maximum amount that can be paid to a service provider during the funding period Once the RFS is approved, an authorization number will be issued to the chosen service provider. * Service delivery can begin only after an authorization number has been received by the service provider. The authorization number confirms that services have been approved through the BC Children and Youth Disability Benefit (CYDB) program and helps prevent families from incurring costs for services that have not been authorized.   Authorization Number A billing authorization number is required for all invoices submitted for payment through the B.C. Children and Youth Disability Benefit Program. Before Service Delivery Wait to receive an authorization number before beginning service delivery. A new authorization number will be issued for each funding period. Starting services only after an authorization number has been issued helps ensure: services are authorized for payment through CYDB; and families are not responsible for costs related to unauthorized services. Changes to Approved Funding During a funding period, families may request changes to approved rates or funding amounts associated with an authorization number by submitting a Request to Amend (RTA) Services Authorization.   Getting Paid: Invoicing When to Invoice and What to Include Invoice monthly for authorized services provided. Only services that have already been delivered are eligible for payment. Invoices must be submitted within six (6) months of the service date. To support timely processing, please ensure that all invoices are complete and include all information reflected in the Sample Invoice available on the forms website . Providing complete invoices helps avoid processing delays and invoices being returned for correction. Invoices for future-dated services, cancelled appointments, and missed appointments will not be processed. Please provide families with copies of all submitted invoices. If an invoice cannot be processed, both the family and service provider will be notified. Invoicing Restrictions Service providers may only invoice for services that have been delivered and received by the child, youth, or family. Invoices must accurately document the date, duration, and nature of the service provided. The following cannot be charged to a family's funding allocation: Future-dated services. Cancelled appointments. Missed appointments. Best practice for Service providers is to maintain a clear cancellation and missed appointment policy and review it with families before services begin. The policy should outline attendance expectations, any grace allowances (such as a limited number of missed appointments that will not result in a fee), and the circumstances under which families may be responsible for missed appointment fees. Missed appointments cannot be invoiced to government funding programs. Any fees associated with missed appointments must be managed directly between the service provider and the family and cannot be charged to the child's funding allocation. Payments Invoices from service providers must be payable within Canada. Payment will be made to service providers by way of either: A direct deposit into a Canadian bank (also known as Electronic Funds Transfer); or A cheque mailed directly to a Canadian address that is not also the address of the child/youth’s parent/guardian. Payments are typically deposited within 3–5 business days following processing. Providers are responsible for protecting personal and confidential information when submitting documents by email.   Reimbursement: Request for Equipment, Supplies, Travel and Training (RESTT) Families may use up to 20% of the Annual Funding for equipment, supplies, travel, and training-related expenses to support accessibility, participation, or effective engagement in services.  All equipment, supplies, travel and training require approval and a Request for Equipment, Supplies, Travel and Training (RESTT) form to be submitted to the Ministry before the expense is incurred unless designated as “pre-approved” items. Pre-approved items are set out in the Children and Youth Disability Benefit Invoicing Pathway Operational Policy for Agreement Signatories. Role of Service Professionals for RESTT Eligible B.C. service professionals, as outlined in the Children and Youth Disability Benefit Invoicing Pathway Operational Policy for Agreement Signatories, may support families in completing a RESTT form when funding is requested for equipment, supplies, travel or training that require ministry approval. The RESTT form allows a service provider to: Outline the requested expense; and Explain how the expense will support the child or youth's disability-related needs, participation, and outcomes. For information on guiding principles and approval criteria see the Children and Youth Disability Benefit Invoicing Pathway Operational Policy for Agreement Signatories : [website link]. Families must receive approval for their submitted RESTT request before purchasing items or incurring expenses, unless the item is pre-approved. Where Can You Learn More? Families receiving Under Age 6 Autism Funding must use service providers listed on the RASP. Qualified professionals interested in providing services to families using their child’s under 6 Autism Funding may apply to be included on the registry. Professionals currently eligible for registration include: Behaviour Consultants and Analysts (Category A and B) Speech-Language Pathologists (Non-Supervised and Supervised) Occupational Therapists (Non-Supervised and Supervised) Physical Therapist. The information below outlines the eligibility requirements, application process, and documentation required to apply for inclusion on the RASP.   Apply to become a RASP professional To become a RASP professional, you must meet the  application requirements for your professional category . You must also register for a Basic BCeID before you can complete your online application. It only takes a few minutes. If you’ve logged into other B.C. government services before, you may already have an account you can use. If you do not already have a Basic BCeID, apply for one before applying to become a RASP professional.  If you encounter any issues applying for your Basic BCeID, please contact the Service BC Help Desk.  Service BC Help Desk is open Monday to Friday (except statutory holidays) from 7:30 am to 5 pm PST, or 8:30 am to 6 pm MST.  1-888-356-2741  (Canada and USA toll-free)  604-660-2355  (Within the lower mainland or outside Canada and USA)  Contact Service BC Help Desk using  their online form .
