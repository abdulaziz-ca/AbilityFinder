 1. Early Years Family Resource Centres
 Early Years Family Resource Centres provide a single location where families with young children can access reliable information, programs and services to support their children's development and family wellness.
Benefits
Some of the information and resources that can be found at Early Years Family Resource Centres include:
 - Early learning opportunities for your child through play-based activity, family groups, art, music, movement or other preschool level activities.
 - Parent education through formal and informal learning opportunities, drop-in programs and seminars.
 - Family wellness and support initiatives to assist your family to identify and build skills that support child development and overall family health and wellness.
 - Information and referrals to link your family to the best possible service to enable you to support your child's development and links to other places in your community that may be able to help meet your family's needs.
Learn more about the benefits of Early Years Family Resource Centres
Eligibility
Anyone with children under the age of six years may access an Early Years Family Resource Centre.

 2. Early Childhood Intervention Program
 The Early Childhood Intervention Program (ECIP) provides home-based specialized services and support to families with children who are experiencing developmental delay or disability or are at risk of experiencing development delays. Examples of these services include:
 - child assessment;
 - developmental strategies;
 - information;
 - case co-ordination;
 - transition support; and
 - referrals.
Benefits
This program helps to improve your child's development by measuring their abilities and provides appropriate activities you can do at home. The program will help you understand the delay or disability and the impact it has on your family. The program also provides connection to other services to support the parent as the child transitions into school or other programming.
Eligibility
Children experiencing developmental delays or disabilities and children who may be at risk of experiencing developmental delays are eligible for ECIP services.
These delays may be:
 - Cognitive, such as challenges problem-solving or reasoning;
 - Physical, such as challenges walking or using utensils;
 - Social, such as challenges interacting with others;
 - Emotional, such as challenges coping with negative emotions; or
 - Communicative, such as challenges talking or understanding speech.
Children do not require a diagnosis to start ECIP services. Participation can begin as soon as the family notices a delay in development which can be as early as birth. Parents may self-refer or they may be referred by others such as health care professionals or educators, with the family's consent.
Contacting ECIPs Near You
 ECIP is delivered in 14 regions across Saskatchewan, by 12 community-based organizations and two school divisions.

 3. KidsFirst Targeted
 KidsFirst is a voluntary program that helps families in vulnerable situations who would benefit from help in supporting their children's development. The program enhances parenting knowledge, provides support and connects families to programs and services that support early development.
Services include:
 - home-visiting services;
 - mental health and addiction services;
 - community connection;
 - early learning opportunities; and 
 - family support.
Benefits
The program helps parents build their skills and knowledge in parenting young children, access to social emotional supports and connects families to other programs and services in the community.
Eligibility
Parents and their children (three years of age and under) who are experiencing family stress, struggling with life changes or who feel they need parenting support and live in a community listed below.

 4. Mobile Early Learning
 Mobile Early Learning provides early childhood group events and resources to families in smaller urban and rural communities across the province where early learning options may be limited.
Services include:
 - Early learning programming such as early childhood development fairs, early learning preschool groups, literacy events like story walks and welcome baby events;
 - Information sessions and clinics on early childhood topics; and
 - Public awareness material on early childhood development
Benefits
 - This program is focused on making early learning more accessible and growing the skills of parents.
 - Mobile Early Learning makes it easy for families to access early learning and child development services in their own community.
 - Children and their caregivers can learn and grow together. 
Eligibility
Parents and their children up to age six living in small cities and rural communities.
More information on Mobile Early Learning programming can be found by visiting the following social media pages or contacting the Mobile Early Learning Community Developers.
