 Premiums and co-payments
 Premiums and co-payments for the New Brunswick Drug Plan are based on household income and reassessed each year.

 Overview
 Premiums and maximum co-payments for the New Brunswick Drug Plan are based on annual family or household income, as indicated by a Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) tax return for the previous year. 
All plan members must pay a 30% co-payment up to a maximum amount per prescription. 
 Premiums and co-payments
 All adult plan members pay a monthly premium based on their family income and the following criteria: 
- if two adults in the family enrol in the plan, then two premiums of the same amount must be paid 
- if only one adult enrols in the plan, only one premium must be paid; however, it is still based on the family or household income  
- eligible dependants will not pay premiums, but at least one parent or guardian must enrol and pay a premium for one or more eligible dependants to be covered 
Premium payment is due on the first day of the coverage period. Monthly premiums will be automatically deducted from the provided bank account each month.

 Household income
 Premiums and co-payments are based on family income.
Income means the total income as declared on line 15000 of the Income Tax Return, minus any elected split-pension amount on line 11600 (if applicable). The CRA determines which sources of income must be included in the amount reported on line 15000.
If an applicant or their spouse does not file a tax return or consent to verify income is not received, the maximum annual premium will be charged. It is important to file a tax return each year so that the premium corresponds with the family income.
New Brunswick Drug Plan premiums and co-payments are considered eligible medical expenses that can be claimed on an income tax return. Premium statements for the previous year will be mailed by the New Brunswick Drug Plan before the end of February.
For further information, contact the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) at 1-800-959-8281.
 Overdue premiums
 If premium payments are not received in full each month, coverage will be suspended or cancelled, and prescription drug claims will not be paid. 
If coverage is suspended, an official notice of suspension will be sent in the mail. Suspended members will have 30 days from the suspension date to pay the amount owing in full. Drug claims will not be paid while coverage is suspended. If after 30 days payment has not been received, coverage will be cancelled.  Members and their spouse will be responsible to pay any amount owing. 
To re-enroll in the New Brunswick Drug Plan at a future date, a reinstatement fee and a waiting period could apply, and all outstanding amounts owing must be paid in full. 
Overdue premiums can be paid using one of the following methods:

For assistance with online banking, contact the financial institution. 
 Reassessment
 A reassessment of premiums and maximum co-payments will occur annually based on family income, as indicated on the CRA tax return for the previous year. However, a reassessment of premiums can be requested at any time, except during the annual reassessment period from August 1 to October 31. 
The New Brunswick Drug Plan does not refund or charge members for differences between monthly premiums before or after unscheduled reassessments. 
The New Brunswick Drug Plan is reviewed annually to determine if adjustments to the premiums, co-payments or income levels are required to account for factors such as prescription drug costs and inflation. This annual review and adjustments help keep the Plan affordable for members while safeguarding its long-term sustainability. Any changes are effective November 1.
