What happens after I’m approved?
You or your Substitute Decision Maker will hire your own care providers and manage your
own care plan based on your assessed needs. In addition, you may appoint a third party
“Care Manager” to act on your behalf in carrying out these tasks.
Once the agreement is signed and the service has started, the Department of Seniors and
Long-Term Care will deposit the approved funding into your bank account each month.
If you are receiving funding more than $500/month, you must submit all receipts for
services to the Nova Scotia Health Authority or our third-party partner on a quarterly basis.
If your funding is less than or equal to $500/month, you are not required to submit receipts
quarterly. However, you must keep them on hand for your own benefit.
Please speak to your Care Coordinator if you have questions.

What if my health status has changed?
If your health status has changed, notify your Care Coordinator immediately. Your Care
Coordinator will reassess your care needs. Program funding may be adjusted, paused, or
stopped if your care needs change.
The Department of Seniors and Long-Term Care may stop funding if you do not meet the
requirements set out in your funding agreement.

What is my responsibility as an employer?
As a direct fund recipient and employer for your care provider, you must fulfill all legal
obligations of an employer. Some helpful resources are listed below:
Your employee must be legally entitled to work in Canada and have a valid SIN. The IRCC
webpage (canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work- canada/hire-
temporary-foreign.html) has the most up-to-date requirements for hiring foreign workers.
For taxation, payroll, and human resources related obligations, review the Government of
Canada’s Hire and Managing Employee guide (canada.ca/en/services/business/hire.html).
For provincial Workers Compensation related questions, the WCB has prepared the
Employer Information Guide (employerguide.wcb.ns.ca/) to help new employers
navigate through various requirements. You can also call the worker safety hotline at
1-800-9LABOUR (1-800-952-2687) for more information.
Requirements under the Occupational Health and Safety Act can be found in Workplace
Health And Safety Regulations (novascotia.ca/just/regulations/regs/ohsworkplace.
htm#TOC1_21).
Caregivers Nova Scotia publishes The Caregiver’s Handbook (caregiversns.org/resources/
handbook/) containing helpful resources for planning care in your home. They also have a
resource page (caregiversns.org/resources/home-care-and-other-services/) for people
receiving care in their home.
