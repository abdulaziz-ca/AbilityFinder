Ontario · Money & taxes

Ontario Trillium Benefit

A tax-free payment that helps low-to-moderate income Ontario residents pay for energy costs, sales tax and property tax. It combines three credits, and you need to be eligible for at least one.

What it is

The three credits are paid together for the 2026 benefit year (July 2026 to June 2027).

  • Northern Ontario Energy Credit: up to $189 if single, up to $290 for families.
  • Energy and Property Tax Credit: up to $290 if you live on a reserve or in a public long-term care home.
  • Sales Tax Credit: up to $378, plus up to $378 for a spouse and each dependent child under 19.
  • Amounts depend on your age, income, residence, family size and rent or property tax.

Good to know

You apply by filing your tax return and the ON-BEN form, even with no income. The Canada Revenue Agency decides if you are eligible. The last day to file is April 30.

What you must meet

The program requires at least one of these:

  • Northern Ontario Energy Credit: you lived in Northern Ontario on December 31, 2025.
  • Ontario Energy and Property Tax Credit: you lived in Ontario on December 31, 2025.
  • Ontario Sales Tax Credit: you are an Ontario resident, 19 or older, or have a partner or child.

Each credit also has conditions, such as age, family status, and rent or property tax paid in 2025.

Amount or value

For the 2026 benefit year: up to $1,307 (age 18 to 64) or $1,488 (65 or older) from the energy and property tax credit, plus other credits

How to apply

  1. File your income tax and benefit return every year.
  2. Fill out and enclose the ON-BEN application form from your tax package.
  3. The Canada Revenue Agency tells you if you are entitled to the credits.
  4. Choose how you want to be paid if you qualify for more than $500.

What you may need

  • Your income tax return
  • Form ON-BEN

Practical tips

  • If you are turning 18 before June 2027, file your tax return to be considered.
  • If you cannot prepare your return yourself, the Community Volunteer Income Tax Program may help.
  • If you file late, you still receive the benefit, but your payments may be delayed.
  • Beware of text messages claiming you are owed a payment; the Government of Ontario will not send them.
