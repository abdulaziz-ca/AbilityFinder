Ontario · Home support

Home and community care (Ontario Health atHome)

Care at home for people of all ages who need it, including seniors and people with disabilities. It also covers respite so a caregiver can take a break. Ontario Health atHome decides what you are eligible for.

What it is

Ontario Health atHome provides home care and links you to community support services.

  • Nursing, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology, social work and healthy eating help.
  • Personal support such as bathing, dressing, eating and toileting.
  • Homemaking such as cleaning, laundry, shopping and meal planning.
  • Respite care at home, in a long-term care home for a short stay, or at an adult day program.

Good to know

If you qualify, the Ontario government pays for home care. Community support services can have a client co-payment. Respite at home is fully covered if you qualify, otherwise other providers charge a fee.

What you must meet

You must meet all of these:

  • You need care at home, at school or in the community.
  • Ontario Health atHome assesses that you are eligible for the services.
  • You have a valid Ontario health card (for respite at home).
  • Your home is properly equipped for the services you need (for respite at home).

Homemaking needs personal support, or constant supervision due to cognitive impairment or acquired brain injury.

Amount or value

Government pays for home care if you qualify; community support services may have a co-payment

How to apply

  1. Call Ontario Health atHome or use their map to find care near you.
  2. A case manager or care coordinator assesses your needs and eligibility, and may visit your home.
  3. If you are eligible, you and anyone you choose make a home care plan.
  4. Your care is scheduled with Ontario Health atHome or a service provider.

What you may need

  • Ontario health card

Practical tips

  • A short respite stay in long-term care can be up to 60 days, and no more than 90 days in a calendar year.
  • Adult day programs need a referral from Ontario Health atHome and usually charge a fee per day.
  • If you are refused, you can complain to Ontario Health atHome or appeal to the Health Services Appeal and Review Board.
