Homeowner Residential Adaptations for Disabilities (RAD)

Low income homeowners can get a forgivable loan of up to $16,000 to make the home more accessible for a household member with a disability or limitation.

Manitoba is new here: please double-check before you rely on a detail

We added Manitoba in October 2026, while some of its programs are changing. Each program here comes from its official page, but some details are not published yet, so confirm with the program before you count on an amount or rule.

  • Manitoba is moving people with a disability from Employment and Income Assistance (EIA) to the new Manitoba Supports for Persons with Disabilities program, and that program's page does not publish payment amounts yet.
  • Since September 1, 2026, EIA covers only emergency dental care and points people to the federal Canadian Dental Care Plan.
  • Some income limits are old (home adaptation limits are from 2019) or change every year (Rent Assist and Winnipeg programs).
  • Winnipeg Transit Plus has proposed new eligibility rules that are not in effect.
  • City programs are listed for Winnipeg only so far.

What it is

The loan pays for permanent changes that relate directly to the disability.

  • Winnipeg limits (2019) are $40,500 without dependants and $54,000 with dependants.
  • The loan is forgiven monthly over five years.
  • You repay it only if you sell, rent out, transfer or leave the home.
  • A program inspector decides which changes qualify.

Good to know

The limit is $19,000 north of the 53rd parallel. Income limits on the page are marked effective January 1, 2019.

What you must meet

You must meet all of these:

  • You are a low income homeowner.
  • A household member has a disability or limitation that makes daily activities difficult.
  • Your household gross income is at or below the limit set for your community.
  • The home is your main residence in Manitoba, outside First Nations communities.

Gross income counts everyone 18 or older in the home, before deductions.

What this guide can't tell you: whether you will be approved. The program decides, often using details we do not ask for.

How to apply

  1. Contact Manitoba Housing, Housing Delivery Branch.
  2. Submit the application with the medical form.
  3. Wait for loan approval before any work starts.
  4. Approved work should be finished within six months of final approval.

What you may need

  • Application
  • Medical form verifying the limitation or disability

Practical tips

  • Work done before loan approval is not covered.
  • Payments are made when work is complete, with progress payments possible; advances are not allowed.
  • Work must meet building codes and standards.
