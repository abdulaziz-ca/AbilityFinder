British Columbia · Home support

Home Support (Home and Community Care)

Publicly subsidized help at home from community health workers with personal care such as bathing, dressing and mobility. It can also give a caregiver a break.

What it is

Home support helps you stay independent and live at home as long as possible.

  • Help covers mobility, nutrition, lifts and transfers, bathing and dressing, cueing, grooming and toileting.
  • It may also include clean-up, laundry of soiled bedding or clothing, and meal preparation.
  • Hours are set by your health, your supports, your care goals and the risk to staff.
  • It is usually provided over months or years, or short term after a hospital discharge.

Good to know

Short-term care may not need the three-month residency. You pay no daily rate if you get GIS, support or shelter allowance, or a War Veterans Allowance.

What you must meet

You must meet all of these:

  • A health authority professional assessed you as needing personal assistance or respite for your caregiver.
  • You agree to pay the assessed client rate.

You must also meet the general eligibility criteria for home and community care services.

Amount or value

Subsidized service; daily rate based on income, and no more than $300 a month with earned income

How to apply

  1. Contact the home and community care office of your health authority, or ask a health care professional to refer you.
  2. Have a health authority professional do a clinical assessment of your need.
  3. Complete the financial assessment with your responsible assessor.
  4. Confirm that you accept the assessed client rate.

What you may need

  • The page does not list documents. Ask your health authority what to bring.

Practical tips

  • Tell your responsible assessor about any earned income when you do your financial assessment.
  • If the daily rate would cause serious financial hardship, you can ask for a temporary reduction.
  • If you and your spouse both get home support, you are both assessed, but only one is charged per service day.
  • If your assessed daily rate is more than the actual cost, you pay no more than the actual cost.
