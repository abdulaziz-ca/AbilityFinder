Community Living BC (CLBC) Adult Supports

A provincial agency that funds lifelong supports for adults 19+ with developmental disabilities. Supports include home sharing and staffed living, employment services, community inclusion, respite and family support. Youth can apply from age 16. What funded support you get, and when, depends on how urgent your situation is and the funding CLBC has at the time of the request.

What it is

Services are delivered through a network of contracted agencies and planned individually with a CLBC facilitator.

Good to know

There are two eligibility streams. Developmental Disability needs a psychologist's cognitive and adaptive assessments. The Personalized Supports Initiative needs an FASD or ASD diagnosis plus significant daily-living limitations. You do not need an intellectual disability for this stream. These assessments are sometimes already done when you were in school, so ask before arranging new ones.

What you must meet

The program requires at least one of these:

  • You are an adult 19 or older with significantly impaired intellectual and adaptive functioning that began before age 18.
  • Under the PSI stream, you are an adult with an FASD or autism spectrum diagnosis plus significant adaptive-functioning limitations.

You can apply from age 16, and supports continue beyond 65.

Amount or value

Individually planned funded services — residential, employment, community inclusion, respite

What it covers

CLBC funds and coordinates:

  • Residential supports (living in your own place, shared living/home sharing with a contracted family, or staffed homes)
  • Employment support to find and keep a job
  • Community inclusion and connection programs
  • Skill development
  • Respite for families
  • Wellness supports for daily responsibilities
  • Behavioural support
  • The Provincial Assessment Centre's mental-health services for people 14+ with developmental disabilities

How to apply

  1. From age 16, ask your MCFD/CYSN worker about transition planning to CLBC
  2. Contact your nearest CLBC office (1-877-660-2522) — a facilitator explains the required documents and approved assessors
  3. Submit assessments and forms completed by an approved professional (registered psychologist for the DD stream); school-years assessments may be reusable
  4. Once eligibility is confirmed, attend the CLBC Welcome Workshop series and work with your facilitator on a support plan
  5. CLBC connects you to funded services based on the plan and available resources

What you may need

  • Cognitive/psychoeducational assessment by a registered psychologist (DD stream)
  • Adaptive functioning assessment completed by an approved professional
  • Confirmed FASD or autism spectrum diagnosis for the PSI stream
  • CLBC eligibility forms completed by approved professionals

Practical tips

  • Start at 16, because assessments and planning take time. What funded support you get, and when, depends on how urgent your situation is and the funding CLBC has at the time of your request.
  • If you were assessed in school, ask the school district for copies. You may not need a new assessment.
  • Not eligible under the DD stream? Ask specifically about the Personalized Supports Initiative (PSI)
  • Respite or equivalent funding is commonly the first service offered to families of transitioning youth. Ask your facilitator what is available in your region.
  • Adults on CLBC services usually also qualify for BC PWD disability assistance. Apply for both.

Timing

When funded support starts depends on how urgent your situation is and the amount of funding CLBC has at the time of the request.
