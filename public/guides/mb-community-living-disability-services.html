Manitoba · Community supports

Community Living disABILITY Services (CLDS)

Supports eligible adults with intellectual disabilities to live in their communities. It can include a person-centred plan about your goals.

Manitoba is new here: please double-check before you rely on a detail

We added Manitoba in October 2026, while some of its programs are changing. Each program here comes from its official page, but some details are not published yet, so confirm with the program before you count on an amount or rule.

  • Manitoba is moving people with a disability from Employment and Income Assistance (EIA) to the new Manitoba Supports for Persons with Disabilities program, and that program's page does not publish payment amounts yet.
  • Since September 1, 2026, EIA covers only emergency dental care and points people to the federal Canadian Dental Care Plan.
  • Some income limits are old (home adaptation limits are from 2019) or change every year (Rent Assist and Winnipeg programs).
  • Winnipeg Transit Plus has proposed new eligibility rules that are not in effect.
  • City programs are listed for Winnipeg only so far.

What it is

CLDS follows the Adults Living with an Intellectual Disability Act, which protects your right to make your own decisions.

  • A person-centred plan covers what matters to you and your goals.
  • CLDS can provide services that help you meet your goals.
  • Family, friends and people in your community can also support you.

Good to know

CLDS is voluntary. You can close your file at any time and re-open it later. A report on intellectual functioning is needed to apply.

What you must meet

You must meet all of these:

  • You have significantly impaired intellectual functioning and adaptive behaviour that existed before age 18.
  • You are 18 or older.
  • You need help with personal care or managing your property.
  • You are a Canadian citizen or legally entitled to live and work in Canada permanently.
  • You normally live in Manitoba, not as a visitor, transient or student from elsewhere.

With registered treaty status, you first need an established permanent off-reserve residence in Manitoba.

What this guide can't tell you: whether you will be approved. The program decides, often using details we do not ask for.

How to apply

  1. Contact the Winnipeg or Rural intake office.
  2. Send your CLDS Application Form with a report on intellectual functioning and adaptive behaviour.
  3. If you are refused, you can appeal to the Social Services Appeal Board.

What you may need

  • CLDS Application Form
  • Report from a registered or school psychologist

Practical tips

  • Your school, family, an agency or a doctor can apply for you with your consent.
  • Winnipeg intake emails go to the address on the page.
