Answer a few questions to see benefits you may qualify for

Federal

  • Disability Tax Credit (DTC) — A non-refundable tax credit that may lower income tax for an approved person or an eligible supporting family member. You also need approval for several related federal programs.
  • Canada Disability Benefit — A monthly payment for working-age adults with a disability and lower income.
  • Child Disability Benefit — A tax-free monthly amount added to the Canada Child Benefit for a child approved for the DTC.
  • Registered Disability Savings Plan (RDSP) — A savings account where the government may add matching grants (up to 300%) and bonds, depending on your family income and how much is contributed — you don't even need to contribute to get the bond if your income is low.
  • Canada Workers Benefit — Disability Supplement — If you work and earn a lower income, this adds an extra disability top-up to your tax refund.
  • CPP Disability Benefit — A monthly payment if a severe, long-term disability stops you from working and you've paid into CPP.
  • CPP Children's Benefit — A monthly payment for the dependent child of someone getting a CPP disability benefit. The disability is the parent's, not the child's. The child does not need to have a disability at all. A child can be paid a maximum of two children's benefits.
  • Canada Student Grant for Students with Disabilities — Extra grant money for post-secondary students with a documented disability.
  • Canada Student Grant — Services & Equipment — Helps pay for education-related services or equipment you need for your studies, if you qualify.
  • Home Accessibility Tax Credit (HATC) — A non-refundable federal credit for home accessibility renovations. The person must be eligible for the Disability Tax Credit at any time in the year, or be 65 or older at year end. Certain family members may claim it for them.
  • Multigenerational Home Renovation Tax Credit (MHRTC) — A refundable federal credit for building a self-contained secondary unit in a home. It lets a senior, or an adult eligible for the Disability Tax Credit, live with a relative. It is worth 14.5% of up to $50,000 of eligible costs, to a maximum of $7,250 for the 2025 tax year.
  • Excise Gasoline Tax Refund — If you have a permanent mobility impairment and cannot safely use public transportation, you can claim back part of the federal excise tax on the gasoline you buy. A qualified medical practitioner has to certify the impairment.
  • Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) — A federal plan that pays part of the cost of dental care. It is for people without private dental coverage whose adjusted family net income is under $90,000. It is based on income, not disability. Canada lists it among its disability benefits because many disabled people have no workplace dental coverage.
  • Disability Supports Deduction (line 21500) — You may be able to deduct support costs if you have an impairment in physical or mental functions. Supports must let you work, attend school or do research for which you received a grant. Only the person with the disability can claim it.
  • Medical Expense Tax Credit (lines 33099 and 33199) — The federal credit for medical expenses you pay yourself. Disability-related costs are often the largest medical expenses a household has. Most of them are claimed here.
  • Canada Caregiver Credit (CCC) — A federal non-refundable credit for someone who supports a spouse, common-law partner or dependant with a mental or physical impairment. The person giving the support claims it, not the person with the disability.

Provincial

  • Trillium Drug Program — Helps Ontario households with high prescription-drug costs compared with their income. For most people the deductible is about 4% of after-tax household income, paid in four quarterly amounts; once you have paid a quarter's amount, you pay up to $2 for each drug filled or refilled until the end of that quarter.
  • Passport program (adults with a developmental disability) — Funding for adults 18 or older with a developmental disability. It helps them take part in their community and live as independently as possible, and it gives their caregivers a break. It is a reimbursement program: you send in receipts and are repaid up to your annual allocation.
  • Home and Vehicle Modification Program — Pays for changes to your home or vehicle. It is for children, adults and seniors with a disability that limits mobility. The changes help them keep living safely at home, take part in their community, and avoid losing a job. The Ontario page sends applicants to March of Dimes for eligibility and application details.
  • Special Services at Home (SSAH) — Funding for families caring for a child under 18 with a physical and/or developmental disability. It buys respite for caregivers, support for the child's day-to-day activities, and activities that help the child grow and develop.
  • Assistance for Children with Severe Disabilities (ACSD) — A monthly payment to help families with the extra costs of caring for a child under 18 who has a severe disability. This includes travel to appointments, special shoes and clothes, and parental relief such as respite. Children with severe disabilities may also get drug, dental and vision coverage.
  • Assistive Devices Program (Ontario) — Helps pay for customized equipment and specialized supplies for a long-term physical disability. This includes wheelchairs and other mobility aids, hearing aids, visual aids, prosthetics, respiratory and ventilator equipment, and diabetic and ostomy supplies. Your income is not considered.
  • Ontario Autism Program — Services and supports for children and youth on the autism spectrum in Ontario, until they turn 18. They include foundational family services, caregiver-mediated early years programs, core clinical services, an entry to school program, and urgent response services.
  • Ontario Disability Support Program (ODSP) — income support — Monthly income support for Ontario adults with a disability who are in financial need. It gives money toward basic needs and shelter, plus health benefits including prescription drugs and vision care. The amount depends on your family size, shelter costs and other income.
  • Accessible Parking Permit (Ontario) — A free permit to park in designated accessible spaces. It is issued to a person, not a vehicle. You can only use it when the permit holder is driving or riding in the vehicle. A regulated health professional certifies your condition on the application.
  • Assured Income for the Severely Handicapped (AISH) — Alberta disability income assistance for people whose permanent disability prevents employment.
  • Alberta Disability Assistance Program (ADAP) — Alberta disability income assistance when a severe disability significantly impedes employment, continuously or episodically, but does not prevent it.
  • Alberta Aids to Daily Living (AADL) — Helps pay for equipment and supplies you need for a long-term illness, disability or condition — wheelchairs, hearing aids, breathing supplies and much more.
  • Persons with Developmental Disabilities (PDD) — Support services for adults with a developmental disability — help with daily living, community involvement, and employment.
  • Family Support for Children with Disabilities (FSCD) — Support and funding for Alberta families raising a child under 18 with a disability — respite, aids, counselling, and reimbursement of some costs.
  • Disability Related Employment Supports (DRES) — May fund approved accommodations for an Albertan who is employed or heading into employment. Their documented permanent or long-term disability must create a barrier to education, training, or employment.
  • Alberta Grant for Students with Disabilities — May fund approved disability-related services and equipment when the federal services-and-equipment grant is unavailable or does not cover all approved costs; federal funding is allocated first.
  • Alberta Adult Health Benefit — Health coverage for adults in lower-income households. It covers prescriptions, dental, eye care, and essential diabetic supplies.
  • Alberta Child Health Benefit — Health coverage for children under 18 in lower-income families. There is also a conditional route for 18- and 19-year-olds still in high school through grade 12.
  • Residential Access Modification Program (RAMP) — Alberta grant that pays for permanent changes so you can get into and move around your own home. It is for wheelchair users of any age, seniors using a 4-wheel walker, and people living with certain progressive conditions.
  • Disability Parking Placard — A placard that lets you use accessible parking spaces.
  • Alberta Service Dog Identification Card — The provincial identification card for a qualified service dog team in Alberta. There is no fee, and the card is mailed in about two weeks.
  • Community Access for People in Continuing Care (CAPCC) — Help for adults with disabilities in facility-based continuing care in Alberta. It helps you take part in your community and feel less lonely. A community access coordinator works with you on an individual service plan.
  • Special Needs Housing — Subsidized provincial housing for people with special housing needs. This includes people with developmental disabilities and people with physical challenges. There is no central application. Find a local housing provider and apply to them directly.
  • Persons with Disabilities (PWD) Designation — BC's disability status for adults 18 and over with a severe impairment expected to last at least two years. You need it to get disability assistance.
  • Fair PharmaCare — BC's main drug plan for anyone enrolled in MSP. It pays a share of your eligible prescriptions based on family net income. The lower your income, the sooner and the more it pays. Free to register.
  • MSP Supplementary Benefits — Partial MSP coverage for visits to six kinds of practitioners. It is for people who may be eligible, including lower-income B.C. residents and people enroled with MSP through the At Home Program or as Mental Health Clients. Families deemed eligible may also be eligible for the Healthy Kids Program.
  • PharmaCare Plan C (Income & Disability Assistance) — Automatic drug coverage from the first dollar if you are on BC income, disability or hardship assistance. PharmaCare pays the full cost of eligible drugs and dispensing fees at the pharmacy counter.
  • PharmaCare Plan G (Psychiatric Medications) — Full coverage of certain psychiatric medications for B.C. residents of any age who have both clinical and financial need. Your prescriber applies for you, and you must renew coverage every year.
  • PharmaCare Plan P (BC Palliative Care Benefits) — Full coverage of palliative care medications for B.C. residents of any age who have reached the end stage of a life-threatening illness and want to be cared for at home. A doctor or nurse practitioner registers the patient. The family has nothing to submit.
  • First Nations Health Benefits (FNHA) — Status First Nations people living in B.C. get health coverage through the First Nations Health Authority. It covers prescriptions, medical supplies and equipment, dental, vision, mental health and medical transportation. You enrol by phone with your status number.
  • BC Healthy Kids Program — Helps pay for basic dental care, prescription eyewear and hearing assistance for children under 19 in low-income families not on income, disability or hardship assistance.
  • Medical Transportation Supplement — Helps pay for extraordinary transportation costs to and from essential medical treatment, using the least expensive appropriate transportation.
  • handyDART (BC Transit) — Door-to-door shared-ride transit in BC Transit communities outside Metro Vancouver, for people whose disability prevents them from using fixed-route transit without assistance.
  • Taxi Saver Program (BC Transit) — Half-price taxi coupons for permanently registered handyDART riders, for one-off trips when handyDART does not work.
  • BC Disability Assistance (PWD) — Monthly income support for adults with the Persons with Disabilities (PWD) designation — a support allowance plus shelter allowance, an automatic $52 transportation supplement, health supplements and PharmaCare Plan C coverage.
  • Autism Funding: Under Age 6 — Direct yearly funding to buy autism intervention services (behaviour consultants, therapists, supervised behaviour interventionists) for children under 6 with an autism diagnosis.
  • Autism Funding: Ages 6-18 — Yearly funding for autism intervention, therapies, life-skills programs, camps and out-of-school tutoring for children and youth aged 6 to 18.
  • BC Children and Youth Disability Benefit — New direct funding for children and youth, from age 0 until the last day of their 19th birthday month, who have significant disabilities of any diagnosis (autism, Down syndrome, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability and more). It replaces Autism Funding and the School-Aged Extended Therapies stream.
  • Monthly Nutritional Supplement — Extra monthly money on top of disability assistance for PWD recipients whose severe condition causes chronic, progressive deterioration of health, and who need extra nutrition to avoid serious decline.
  • Optical Supplements (Glasses & Eye Exams) — Eye exams and prescription eyewear for everyone on income or disability assistance. Show your BC Services Card at any eye clinic, and the ministry pays the optometrist or optician directly.
  • BC Bus Pass Program — A bus pass for people on BC disability assistance and for low-income seniors. It works on scheduled BC Transit services across the province. In Metro Vancouver it comes as a Compass Card.
  • Accessible Parking Permit (SPARC BC) — BC's accessible parking placard for people with mobility limitations. Hang it from the rear-view mirror. It is valid whether you drive or ride as a passenger.
  • Medical Equipment & Devices Supplement — The ministry buys, repairs and replaces essential medical equipment — canes to power wheelchairs, hospital beds, transfer aids, pressure-relief mattresses, specialized glucose meters — for people on assistance with no other way to pay.
  • At Home Program: Medical Benefits — Medical equipment, therapies, dental and optical care, medical travel and PharmaCare Plan F for eligible children. See the assessed daily-living, direct nursing care and palliative routes below.
  • Supported Child Development — Community programs that help children with extra support needs take part in regular, fully inclusive child care. They support the family and the child care centre, not just the child.
  • At Home Program: School-Aged Extended Therapies (SAET) — May help families with the cost of out-of-school occupational therapy, physiotherapy and speech-language pathology for school-aged children enrolled in the At Home Program.
  • Community Living BC (CLBC) Adult Supports — A provincial agency that funds lifelong supports for adults 19+ with developmental disabilities. Supports include home sharing and staffed living, employment services, community inclusion, respite and family support. Youth can apply from age 16. What funded support you get, and when, depends on how urgent your situation is and the funding CLBC has at the time of the request.
  • Canada Student Grant for Students with Disabilities (StudentAid BC) — A grant of $2,800 per program year (August 1 to July 31) that you do not repay. It is for post-secondary students with a permanent, or persistent or prolonged, disability. In B.C. it is handled through your StudentAid BC application.
  • Canada Student Grant for Services and Equipment – Students with Disabilities — Up to $20,000 per year for disability-related education services and equipment, such as notetakers, tutors, interpreters and technical aids. It is for post-secondary students with disabilities and goes through StudentAid BC.
  • B.C. Access Grant for Students with Disabilities — Non-repayable grant money that replaces B.C. student loans for full-time students with a disability, so you borrow less.
  • B.C. Supplemental Bursary for Students with Disabilities — A bursary you do not repay, to help with the cost of post-secondary education. It is for students with a permanent, or persistent or prolonged, disability. You may be eligible as a full-time or part-time student if you qualify for the Canada Student Grant for Students with Disabilities.
  • B.C. Assistance Program for Students with Disabilities — Funding for exceptional education-related services and adaptive equipment. Use it after the federal services and equipment grant runs out.
  • Learning Disability Assessment Bursary — Covers the up-front cost of a learning disability assessment. This assessment is what opens the door to disability funding and academic accommodations.
  • B.C. Access Grant for Deaf Students — Helps deaf and hard of hearing students with the extra cost of a specialized school that teaches in American Sign Language. It applies only to Gallaudet University in Washington, D.C. and the National Technical Institute for the Deaf in Rochester, New York. Both are in the United States.
  • Dental Supplements (Income & Disability Assistance) — Basic dental care for people on disability assistance, such as check-ups, fillings, extractions and dentures. The ministry pays the dentist directly. Emergency treatment to relieve pain is covered for everyone on assistance.
  • B.C. Home Owner Grant — additional grant for people with disabilities — A larger property tax grant for B.C. homeowners with a disability, or who live with a relative who has one. It reduces the property tax you owe; it is not a payment.
  • Home Renovation Tax Credit for Seniors and Persons with Disabilities — A refundable B.C. tax credit. It is worth 10% of up to $10,000 in accessibility renovations to your principal residence, so up to $1,000 back per year. It is for people who qualify for the disability tax credit, seniors 65+, and family members who live with them.
  • B.C. Rebate for Accessible Home Adaptations (BC RAHA) — A B.C. rebate that pays back part of the cost of home adaptations for independent living. It is for lower-income households where someone has a permanent disability or loss of physical ability. Homeowners apply on their own. Tenants apply together with their landlord.
  • Work-Able Accessible Employment Program (BC Public Service) — A paid internship program in the BC Public Service for recent post-secondary graduates who self-identify as having a disability. This can be any physical, sensory, neurological, visible or invisible disability.
  • WorkBC Assistive Technology Services — Funding for assistive technology you need to get or keep a job. This includes devices and equipment, ergonomic furniture, communication and hearing devices, ASL interpreting and captioning, and workplace modifications. It even includes vehicle modifications for getting to work.
  • WorkBC Employment Services — BC's free public employment service. Help finding work, possible funding support for training, and possible financial supports, with services built specifically for people with disabilities.
  • Fuel Tax Refund for Persons with Disabilities — Refunds the provincial fuel tax on a vehicle you own, lease or have an ownership interest in. If you are eligible for the program, you may also receive 25% off your basic ICBC insurance.
  • ICBC Disability Discount (Basic Autoplan) — If you are eligible for the provincial fuel tax refund program for persons with disabilities, you may receive 25% off the basic part of your ICBC vehicle insurance.
  • Property Tax Deferment for Persons with Disabilities — Instead of you paying your property taxes each year, the province pays them and registers a loan against your home. You repay when you sell or transfer it.
  • B.C. Sales Tax Credit — A small refundable credit you claim on your tax return. It is not a disability program. It helps people with a low income who file a return.
  • Ontario Child Benefit — A monthly payment for low-income to moderate-income families to help with the cost of raising children. The Canada Revenue Agency sets the amount for each family.
  • Developmental Services Ontario adult services — Developmental Services Ontario (DSO) is the way in to provincially funded services for adults with a developmental disability. Staff help you apply, assess your needs and connect you with available supports.
  • Enhanced Respite for medically fragile children — Pays families back for respite care for a child under 18 who is medically fragile or technology dependent and needs care 24 hours a day. You claim the cost of covered respite services or supports.
  • Guaranteed Annual Income System (GAINS) — A monthly, non-taxable payment for Ontario seniors with low income. It is paid on top of any federal OAS and GIS payments you may get.
  • Healthy Smiles Ontario — A government-funded dental program for children and youth 17 and under from low-income households. It pays for preventive, routine and emergency dental services.
  • Ontario Works — Money for living costs such as food and rent, plus health benefits, for people in financial need. In most cases you take part in employment-related activities to get the money.
  • Ontario Seniors Dental Care Program — Free, routine dental care for low-income Ontario seniors 65 or older. It covers check-ups, fillings, x-rays, tooth removal and treatment for infection, pain and gum disease.
  • Ontario Trillium Benefit — A tax-free payment that helps low-to-moderate income Ontario residents pay for energy costs, sales tax and property tax. It combines three credits, and you need to be eligible for at least one.
  • Ontario Drug Benefit — Covers most of the cost of prescription drugs on the Ontario Drug Benefit Formulary. You can qualify at 65, or sooner through social assistance, home care and other routes.
  • Home and community care (Ontario Health atHome) — Care at home for people of all ages who need it, including seniors and people with disabilities. It also covers respite so a caregiver can take a break. Ontario Health atHome decides what you are eligible for.
  • Northern Health Travel Grant — Helps Northern Ontario residents pay part of the cost of travelling at least 100 km one way for specialist care. It does not cover all travel costs, such as meals or taxis.
  • ODSP employment supports and earnings rules — If you get Ontario Disability Support Program (ODSP) income support, you can work and keep your support and benefits. Extra benefits help with child care and start-up costs for a job, training or a business.
  • ODSP health and disability benefits (including Special Diet Allowance) — Health and disability benefits for people who get Ontario Disability Support Program (ODSP) income support, and their families. They help with drugs, dental, vision, assistive devices, medical travel and special diets.
  • Ontario Electricity Support Program (OESP) — A monthly credit on your electricity bill for lower-income households. The amount depends on how many people live in the home and your combined after-tax household income.
  • Choice in Supports for Independent Living (CSIL) — Your health authority sends you money each month so you can hire and manage your own home support workers. For people with a significant physical disability who want more control.
  • B.C. Family Benefit — A tax-free monthly payment for families with children under 18. The CRA pays it together with the Canada child benefit and works out the amount from your tax return.
  • Home Support (Home and Community Care) — Publicly subsidized help at home from community health workers with personal care such as bathing, dressing and mobility. It can also give a caregiver a break.
  • Income Assistance — Monthly help for people in need who have no other resources, meant to support a move into work. It is a different program from disability assistance (PWD).
  • Nursing Support Services (BC Children's Hospital) — Scheduled in-home nursing respite so families get a break, and trained school staff who help children with complex medical needs do their care at school.
  • Persons with Persistent Multiple Barriers (PPMB) — An income assistance category for people with a long-term health condition and other barriers to work. You have no job search duties, and your family may get a higher rate and extra supplements.
  • Rental Assistance Program (RAP) — Monthly help with private-market rent for low-income families with at least one dependent child. A child of any age can count if they have a disability as defined by the CRA.
  • Shelter Aid for Elderly Renters (SAFER) — A monthly rent supplement for B.C. residents aged 60 or older with a low to moderate income who rent in the private market.
  • Hearing Instruments and Alternative Hearing Assistance Supplement — Pays for the least expensive, appropriate hearing aids, repairs and supplies for eligible recipients who lack the money. A separate monthly supplement helps with profound hearing loss in both ears.
  • Alberta Blue Cross Non-Group Coverage — Extra health coverage for Albertans under 65 and their dependants. It helps pay for prescriptions, diabetes supplies, ambulance and more. You pay a monthly premium.
  • Alberta Brain Injury Initiative — A network of agencies that helps adults with an acquired brain injury, and their families, find and use supports. A service coordinator looks at your needs and helps build a plan.
  • Alberta Child and Family Benefit — Payments for lower and middle-income families with children under 18. Getting AISH, Income Support or the Alberta child care subsidy does not stop you from getting it.
  • Home and Community Care (continuing care at home) — Health and personal care at home for people of any age, including help with dressing, bathing, meals and wound care. It also offers respite for caregivers.
  • Alberta Income Support — Money for basic needs like food, clothing and shelter if you cannot pay for them. It can also include health benefits, employment services and help getting child support.
  • Rent Assistance Benefit — A long-term rent subsidy for Albertans with low income. It is paid directly to tenants and may be renewed each year.
  • Alberta Seniors Benefit — Monthly money for lower-income seniors 65 and older. The amount depends on your income, where you live and whether you have a partner.
  • Dental and Optical Assistance for Seniors — Help paying for basic dental care and prescription glasses for lower-income seniors 65 and older. You must first be approved for Seniors Financial Assistance programs.
  • Special Needs Assistance for Seniors — Money toward set items for lower-income seniors 65 and older, such as CPAP machines, diabetes supplies, prescription costs, clothing, and appliances or furniture.
  • Seniors Home Adaptation and Repair Program (SHARP) — A low-interest home equity loan for seniors to repair or adapt their home, such as stair lifts, walk-in tubs or wider doorways. You do not make monthly repayments.
  • Subsidized housing (BC Housing Registry) for people with disabilities — Long-term rental housing for B.C. residents with disabilities and a low income. Rent is based on your income. Demand is high, so wait times cannot be predicted.
  • Children's Rehabilitation Services (Children's Treatment Centres) — Therapy and family supports for children and youth with physical or developmental disabilities or communication difficulties. You do not need a formal referral.

Municipal

  • Ontario Renovates home repair and accessibility funding (Region of Waterloo) — Limited funding for low- to moderate-income homeowners in Waterloo Region. It pays for eligible home repairs or accessibility changes for people with disabilities. It is a loan that is fully forgiven over 10 years. If accessibility changes are needed, up to $5,000 of the loan is a grant you do not repay.
  • Homemaking Support Services Program (Region of Waterloo) — The Region pays agencies to help eligible people live on their own. Help includes light housekeeping, meal planning and preparation, and laundry. The waitlist is about 2–3 years right now, except in special circumstances.
  • Curbside waste support programs (Region of Waterloo) — Two supports for Waterloo Region residents who make extra medical-related waste, or whose disability or physical limit makes it hard to put black and green carts at the curb. One is assisted cart set-out. The other is a medical exemption with 30 free garbage bag tags a year for approved applicants.
  • Affordable Transit Program (Grand River Transit) — The Affordable Transit Program gives discounted GRT fares to Waterloo Region residents on a low income. Eligibility depends on total household income.
  • CNIB pass (Grand River Transit) — An annual pass for CNIB customers. It gives unlimited use of regular GRT buses and ION light rail. CNIB customers ride free with a special CNIB EasyGO fare card.
  • MobilityPLUS specialized transit (Grand River Transit) — MobilityPLUS runs in the urban areas of Waterloo Region. It is for eligible, registered customers whose disability affects their ability to travel on GRT buses or ION trains.
  • MobilityPLUS taxi coupons (Grand River Transit) — MobilityPLUS customers can buy a taxi coupon book for $30. It holds $60 in $5, $2 and $1 coupons. The coupons have punched holes to show each value.
  • Transit Support Person program (Grand River Transit) — For people who need regular or occasional help while travelling because of a disability. It covers GRT regular buses, MobilityPLUS buses and ION trains. The support person who helps you does not pay a fare.
  • Homemakers Services (York Region) — York Region can help pay for an in-home homemaker. It is for people who are ill or disabled, either for a short time or for good. A homemaker helps with laundry, meals and light housekeeping.
  • Recreation subsidies (York Region) — York Region funds subsidies for recreation and camps. Families who meet one of the Region's listed routes may be eligible. Children can join programs and camps run by their municipality. Overnight camp funding is for youth aged 12 to 17.
  • Community Pass — OC Transpo discount for ODSP recipients (Ottawa) — A discounted OC Transpo pass for Ottawa residents who get ODSP. It covers every route, including Para Transpo, with no top-up charge. The Access pass costs the same but does not include this.
  • Access pass — OC Transpo discount for Para Transpo customers (Ottawa) — A discounted monthly OC Transpo pass for registered Para Transpo customers. It gives unlimited travel on the O-Train and all buses except Para Transpo. It takes about a third off the cash fare on Para Transpo trips.
  • EquiPass — OC Transpo low-income discount (Ottawa) — A discounted OC Transpo pass for Ottawa residents whose family income is below the federal low income threshold, provided they do not already receive transportation benefits from Social Services.
  • a-card — free OC Transpo travel if you are blind or partially sighted (Ottawa) — The a-card lets people who are blind or partially sighted ride OC Transpo free on every route except Para Transpo. You qualify with a CNIB card or an Attorney-General of Ontario Guide Dog photo ID card.
  • Ontario Renovates home repair and accessibility funding (London) — Federal and provincial funding, run by the City of London. It helps lower- and moderate-income homeowners make their home accessible or do essential repairs. They must be seniors aged 60 or older, or people with disabilities.
  • Affordable Pass Program (Windsor) — A fee subsidy for Windsor-Essex residents and households on a low income. For approved applicants, Transit Windsor lists a 30-day bus pass at $63 for adults or $43.50 for youth, before taxes.
  • 90% Recreation Discount (Windsor) — The 90% Recreation Discount is part of the Pathway to Potential Recreation Assistance Program. It gives eligible Windsor-Essex residents 90% off eligible recreation program registration fees, before taxes.
  • HSR Fare Assist (Hamilton) — Half-price single-ride PRESTO fares on Hamilton transit. It is for households below the Low-Income Measure, including people who get ODSP or CPP Disability. It covers accessible DARTS trips and regular buses. It covers everyone eligible in the household.
  • ActiveAssist fee subsidy (Brampton) — A City of Brampton fee subsidy that helps low-income families and individuals join recreation programs. It is added as a credit on your account. Once approved, you qualify for two years.
  • ActiveAssist fee assistance (Mississauga) — Fee assistance that helps Mississauga residents in low-income households, and newcomers with refugee status, join City recreation and culture programs. Each eligible person on the application gets a credit for the year.
  • Ottawa Hand in Hand recreation fee support — Fee support for City of Ottawa recreation and culture programs, for residents with a low income, a family member with a disability, or who already take part in a community or social service agency program. Receiving ODSP or Assistance for Children with Severe Disabilities counts as proof of financial need.
  • Fair Pass Transit Discount (Toronto) — A discount on TTC and Wheel-Trans fares for lower-income Toronto residents. It is loaded onto your PRESTO card for 12 months. If you get ODSP or Ontario Works, you already meet the income test.
  • Welcome Policy recreation fee subsidy (Toronto) — A yearly credit toward City of Toronto recreation programs for lower-income residents. It covers registered programs, instructional swim and sport, camps, after-school care, memberships and multi-use passes.
  • Calgary Fair Entry — Transit & Recreation — One application gets you a low-income monthly transit pass and lower-cost City of Calgary pools, fitness and recreation programs.
  • Edmonton Ride Transit & Leisure Access — A subsidized monthly Arc transit card plus reduced-cost access to City of Edmonton recreation facilities.
  • Red Deer Transit & Recreation Fee Assistance — A reduced monthly transit pass plus help with City recreation fees. If you are on AISH, you may automatically qualify when you show the City your documents.
  • Lethbridge Fee Assistance Program — One City program covers transit, Access-A-Ride paratransit, and recreation fees. Each has a separate application form. AISH counts as proof of income.
  • Medicine Hat Fair Entry — One application, valid up to two years, for cheaper transit passes plus recreation and Esplanade education programs.
  • Grande Prairie AISH Transit Pass & Recreation Access — If you're on AISH, a monthly Grande Prairie transit pass costs $10.25 instead of $74.25. This is the deepest municipal transit discount in the province that we've found.
  • St. Albert Transit & Recreation Subsidy — St. Albert's subsidy gives eligible residents free local fares within St. Albert and free local Handibus tickets for approved riders. AISH and ADAP recipients are eligible for a free annual membership at City-run recreation facilities.
  • Strathcona County Everybody Rides & Everybody Gets to Play — Reduced transit fares on your Arc card and a no-cost annual Active Pass+ for County recreation facilities, for residents on a limited income.
  • Airdrie Fair Access — One income-tested application covering Airdrie transit passes and Genesis Place recreation, at 25%, 50% or 75% off depending on income.
  • Wood Buffalo LIFT (Low-Income Fare Transit) — A $10 monthly transit pass, 75% off specialized SMART Bus passes, and 60% off regional recreation memberships through a community partner.
  • Spruce Grove Low Income Transit Pass — A reduced local or commuter transit pass for qualifying Spruce Grove-area residents.
  • Leduc Transit & Recreation Subsidies — Financial-navigation support that can provide a half-price monthly bus pass. You choose a local, commuter or LATS pass. The Recreation Assistance Program is now closed. It will reopen for new applications in January 2027.
  • Cochrane Connect Card — A Town card for residents facing financial or situational barriers, with local transit and recreation discounts.
  • Okotoks Recreation Fee Assistance — A recreation-fee subsidy for qualifying low-income Okotoks and Foothills County residents.
  • Canmore Affordable Services Program — A Town program that gives qualifying residents lower-cost services, including Regional ROAM Transit and recreation.
