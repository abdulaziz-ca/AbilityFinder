Family Respite Benefit (child with an intellectual disability)

Pays parents or guardians a monthly amount to arrange short-term care for a child under 18 with an intellectual disability. It gives caregivers a break. The amount depends on need.

What it is

Respite gives caregivers a short-term break from daily care of a child.

  • The benefit is paid monthly.
  • Levels depend on financial need and assessed disability impact.
  • It pays for short-term extra or alternative supervision and care.
  • You cannot pay yourself or use it for work-related child care.

Good to know

The benefit is income-tested, and the page gives no dollar amounts. You work with Community Living Service Delivery on a respite plan.

What you must meet

You must meet all of these:

  • You are a Saskatchewan resident.
  • Your child under age 18 lives in your family home.
  • Your child is accepted for Community Living Service Delivery (CLSD) services.
  • You meet the financial eligibility criteria.

You must agree to an annual income test, an annual review and to use the benefit for respite care.

Amount or value

Monthly income-tested benefit; amount set by need and disability impact

How to apply

  1. Contact the nearest Community Living Service Delivery office.
  2. Complete the application and sign the declaration.
  3. Take part in the child and family assessments.
  4. Agree to income checks with the Canada Revenue Agency.
  5. Work with CLSD on your respite plan.

What you may need

  • Signed application with declaration
  • Consent to income verification using your Notice of Assessment
  • You may be asked to provide records

Practical tips

  • Expect two assessments: the child's support needs and the family impact.
  • Report any change in your circumstances.
