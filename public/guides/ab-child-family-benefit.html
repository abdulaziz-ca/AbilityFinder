Alberta · Money (for parents)

Alberta Child and Family Benefit

Payments for lower and middle-income families with children under 18. Getting AISH, Income Support or the Alberta child care subsidy does not stop you from getting it.

What it is

How it works

  • The base part goes to lower-income families, whether or not they earn employment income.
  • The working part starts when family employment income is over $2,760.
  • The working part grows by 15% of each extra dollar earned, up to the maximum.
  • Payments are not taxable and do not change your AISH or Income Support.

Good to know

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) pays in August, November, February and May. The amount depends on your family income and how many children you have.

What you must meet

You must meet all of these:

  • You are a parent of one or more children under 18.
  • You live in Alberta.
  • You file a tax return.
  • You meet the income criteria.

In 2026–27, amounts are reduced once family net income goes over $28,116 or $47,115.

What this guide can't tell you: whether you will be approved. The program decides, often using details we do not ask for.

Amount or value

Base: up to $1,529 (1 child) to $3,821 (4 or more), July 2026 to June 2027. Working part: up to $782 to $2,061.

How to apply

  1. File your tax return each year.
  2. If you qualify for the Canada Child Benefit, you are considered automatically.
  3. If you have not applied for the Canada Child Benefit, apply for it online.

Practical tips

  • Free tax clinics can prepare tax returns for eligible lower-income Albertans.
  • Payments under $10 in a quarter may be joined and paid less often.
  • Use the child and family benefits calculator to estimate your amount. Have your tax return handy.
  • If your family leaves Alberta, you may have to return money for the months you were away.
