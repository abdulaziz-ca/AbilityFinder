BC Disability Assistance (PWD)

Monthly income support for adults with the Persons with Disabilities (PWD) designation — a support allowance plus shelter allowance, an automatic $52 transportation supplement, and full health supplements and PharmaCare Plan C coverage.

What it is

Disability assistance is BC's core income program for people with the PWD designation. A single person receives a $983.50 monthly support allowance plus actual shelter costs up to $500, and every PWD recipient gets a $52 transportation supplement as cash or an in-kind bus pass. It also opens the door to dental, optical, medical equipment, medical transportation and nutrition supplements, and 100% drug coverage under PharmaCare Plan C.

Good to know

Rates effective December 1, 2025, re-verified July 2026 — $1,483.50 single is still the current maximum. You can also work: effective January 1, 2026 you can earn up to $16,200 a year before earnings change your payment, or $23,400 for a couple where one of you has PWD and $32,400 where both do. There is no monthly cap, so you can earn it whenever suits you, and even if you go over the limit you always keep 100% of the money you earn — only the assistance payment is reduced.

Amount or value

Up to $1,483.50/month single ($983.50 support + $500 shelter) plus $52/month transportation supplement; up to $2,662/month for a couple where both have PWD

How to apply

  1. Create an account at myselfserve.gov.bc.ca (or call 1-866-866-0800) and complete the online application so the ministry can check financial eligibility
  2. Request the PWD designation application and complete the applicant section (form HR2883)
  3. Have your doctor or nurse practitioner complete the medical report section
  4. Have a doctor, nurse practitioner or prescribed professional (e.g., occupational therapist, social worker) complete the assessor report
  5. Submit everything and respond to any ministry follow-up; once the designation is approved and you are financially eligible, payments begin
  6. Report income and changes monthly as required to keep payments accurate

What you may need

  • ID and Social Insurance Number for all adults in the family unit
  • Bank statements and details of assets and income
  • Rent receipt, lease or proof of shelter costs
  • Completed PWD application (applicant, medical and assessor sections)

Practical tips

  • Already on CPP Disability, Community Living BC, the At Home Program, PharmaCare Plan P (palliative) or ISC PWD? A simplified prescribed-class application (HR3642) skips most of the medical paperwork
  • The $52 transportation supplement can be taken as a BC Bus Pass instead of cash — choose whichever you actually use
  • PWD has higher asset limits than regular income assistance, so savings do not disqualify you as quickly
  • At 65 most recipients transition to federal Old Age Security and GIS — the ministry will contact you before then
  • The earnings exemption runs January 1 to December 31 and does not carry over. Anything you do not use is gone, and you get a fresh limit each year.
  • There is no monthly limit. You can earn the whole exemption in one busy month or spread it across the year without your assistance changing.
  • Going over the limit does not cost you your earnings. Your assistance is reduced by the amount you went over, and you keep 100% of what you earned.
  • The ministry sends you a letter once you reach 75% of your exemption, and you can check what is left any time on My Self Serve.
  • If you start disability assistance partway through the year, your exemption is prorated for the months left in the year. A change in your family also changes your limit.
  • File monthly report HR0081 by the fifth of the month if you have income or any change to report — and report your income even when it is exactly the same as last month.

Timing

Financial eligibility is usually confirmed within days; the PWD designation decision commonly takes several weeks to a few months once all three report sections are submitted
